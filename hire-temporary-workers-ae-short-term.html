How to Hire Temporary Workers in the UAE for Short-Term Projects

Every business in the UAE faces a time when the current team is not enough. A project gets a tight deadline, a busy season begins, or a few regular staff go on leave. Hiring full-time people for a need that lasts only a few weeks or months is slow and costly, which is why temporary workers are so common here. If you are looking for a manpower supply company in Dubai, or you plan to hire on your own, it helps to understand how the process works first. This guide explains it in simple steps so you can get the right people without breaking any rules.

What Are Temporary Workers in the UAE?

Temporary workers are hired for a short period or for one specific job. Their work ends on a fixed date or when the project is finished. In the UAE, private-sector jobs run on fixed-term contracts, so a temporary role simply has a shorter term, such as three or six months. It can be renewed if both sides agree.

The main difference from permanent staff is that you do not plan to keep them for the long run. On legal matters, though, they are treated almost the same. They need a proper contract, a valid work visa and salary paid on time.

When Should You Hire Temporary Workers?

Temporary hiring works best when your need has a clear end point. Some common situations are:

  • Seasonal demand: Hotels, retail shops, logistics firms and event companies get extra busy during holidays, sales and big events.
  • Short-term projects: Construction, fit-out, maintenance and shutdown work often need more hands only until the job is done.
  • Staff absence: Someone is on leave and the work cannot wait.
  • Testing a role: You want to see if a position is really needed before hiring someone permanently.
  • Special skills: You need a particular trade or skill for a few weeks, not for the whole year.

In these cases, temporary workers let you grow or shrink your team without long-term commitments.

Different Ways to Hire Temporary Workers

There is more than one route, and each has its own pros and cons.

Hiring directly. You find the worker, sponsor the visa, sign the contract and handle payroll yourself. You have full control, but the paperwork and legal responsibility are all yours. For a short project, this can take more time than the work itself.

Hiring freelancers or off-payroll workers. This can look cheap and quick, but it carries real compliance risk. If the worker does not have the right visa or contract, you could face fines and delays.

Using a staffing or manpower supply agency. A licensed agency finds the workers, employs them on paper and sends them to your site. It usually handles the visa, insurance, payroll and documents. You decide the daily tasks and the agency looks after the employment side. For most short-term projects, this is the simplest option.

Using an Employer of Record (EOR). An EOR becomes the legal employer while you manage the work. It is useful for companies that want to hire in the UAE without setting up a local company first.

How to Hire Temporary Workers: Step by Step

1. Define what you need

Before you contact anyone, write down the basics: the role, the number of workers, the skills needed, the start date, how long you need them and where they will work. Clear details save time and avoid mismatches later.

2. Choose your hiring route

Decide whether you will hire directly or go through an agency. If you need workers quickly, or you do not have an HR team, an agency is usually easier.

3. Check the supplier's licence and track record

Temporary staffing is legal in the UAE when the supplier holds a valid manpower supply licence from the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). Ask to see it. Also ask about past projects, how fast they can send workers and how they handle problems on site.

4. Screen the workers

Do not skip checks just because the job is short. Confirm identity, experience and certificates. For skilled trades, ask about trade tests or proof of past work. For site jobs, check that workers have the basic safety training your project needs.

5. Sign a clear contract or agreement

If you hire directly, the worker needs a written contract with the role, pay, working hours and end date. If you use an agency, sign a service agreement that states who is responsible for what, including payroll, insurance, visas and any fines. Do not rely on verbal promises.

6. Confirm the visa, insurance and salary process

Every worker needs a valid work visa and medical insurance. Salaries must be paid on time through the Wages Protection System (WPS). If an agency is involved, ask them to confirm all of this in writing.

7. Onboard them properly

Even for a short stay, a quick induction helps a lot. Show them the site or workplace, explain the safety rules, share the tools and access they need, and name one person they can go to with questions.

8. Review and decide what comes next

Near the end of the term, check how the work went. If you still need the person, extend the contract. If not, close it properly, with final pay and paperwork done on time.

UAE Rules Employers Should Keep in Mind

Temporary workers get the same basic legal protection as permanent staff. These are the main points to remember:

  • Written contract: Every worker needs one, even for a short job.
  • Working hours: The normal limit is 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week, and it is shorter during Ramadan. Work beyond the limit must be paid as overtime.
  • WPS payment: Salaries must go through approved channels on time.
  • Visa and permits: Foreign workers must have a valid work visa sponsored by the employer or the licensed agency.
  • Summer midday break: For outdoor work, the midday break rule applies in the hottest months, and employers must plan shifts around it.
  • Records: Keep copies of contracts, pay slips and other documents. They help if there is ever a dispute.

If you use an agency, ask which of these duties they handle. A good one covers most of them, but you should still know what is happening on your site.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many problems with temporary hiring are easy to prevent. Here are the ones that come up most often:

  • Hiring through unlicensed middlemen. If the supplier has no proper licence, you carry the risk.
  • Starting without a contract. This leads to confusion over pay, hours and duties.
  • Giving vague job details. Workers who do not know what is expected are slow and unsure.
  • Skipping the induction. A short briefing prevents mistakes and accidents.
  • Ignoring safety. Temporary workers need the same protection as everyone else on site.
  • Paying late. Delayed salary can cause legal trouble and hurt the team's mood.
  • Waiting too long to ask. Good workers get booked up in busy seasons. Plan early.

Tips to Get the Best From Temporary Workers

A few simple habits make a big difference. Give clear targets and deadlines from day one. Pair each new worker with a supervisor or a senior team member. Check in regularly so small issues do not grow. Treat temporary staff with the same respect as your permanent team, since people who feel valued usually work better, even on a short stay.

It also helps to keep a small group of reliable workers you can call back for future projects. Familiar workers already know your standards, so they get up to speed much faster.

Final Thoughts

Hiring temporary workers in the UAE does not have to be complicated. Know exactly what you need, pick the right hiring route, check licences and documents, sign clear agreements and follow the labour rules. Do these things and you can scale your team up or down quickly, control your costs and keep your project on schedule. For many businesses, working with a licensed supplier is the easiest way to get skilled people on site without the heavy paperwork.
